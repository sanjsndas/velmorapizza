    The Science of Pizza: Hydration, Heat, and Harmony

    by Dr. Marco Ferretti · 18 min read · updated Aug 2026

    Pizza is not food. It is a system. In this extensive guide, we dissect every variable—from the protein content of flour to the thermodynamics of a stone oven. This is the definitive resource for the obsessive pizza maker.

    1. The Hydration Matrix

    Water is the silent architect. Higher hydration (≥ 65%) yields airy, open crumb but demands a gentle hand. Lower hydration (≤ 55%) gives you a sturdy, crisp base—perfect for heavy toppings. The sweet spot for Neapolitan style is 62–65%.

    Hydration %TextureBest for
    55–58%Firm, crispRoman, thin crust
    62–65%Soft, airyNeapolitan, classic
    68–72%Open crumb, chewyCanotto, gourmet

    2. The Fermentation Ladder

    Cold fermentation (24–72h) develops depth. We recommend 48 hours as the sweet spot for home bakers. Use a preferment like poolish or biga for extra complexity.

    ❓ FAQ: Why does my dough tear?
    Answer: Over-fermentation or weak gluten. Try using a higher protein flour (W ≥ 300) and reduce hydration by 2%. Let it rest for 20 minutes after kneading.
    📊 INFOGRAFIC: The ideal temperature curve for Neapolitan baking (400–450°C, 60–90 seconds)

    3. Common Mistakes & Expert Fixes

    • Mistake: Soggy center → Fix: Bake on a preheated stone, use less sauce, and avoid overtopping.
    • Mistake: Burnt crust, raw interior → Fix: Lower the temperature slightly and extend bake time by 30s.

    4. Comparison: Wood vs. Gas vs. Electric

    Oven typeTempFlavorLearning curve
    Wood-fired400–480°CSmoky, complexHigh
    Gas350–420°CClean, consistentMedium
    Electric (steel)300–380°CNeutralLow

    The choice depends on your style. Wood for theatre, gas for precision, electric for convenience. But remember: the oven is just a tool; the baker is the artist.
